Fungal Infection

ICD-11: 1F00 • Mycosis

Clinical Overview

Fungal infection (mycosis) is an inflammatory skin and mucosal condition caused by opportunistic dermatophytes, Candida species, or systemic fungal pathogens. Characterized by scaling, erythema, and intense pruritus.

Primary Symptoms & Diagnostic Signs

  • Itching and intense cutaneous pruritus
  • Skin rash, scaling, and annular erythematous plaques
  • Nail discoloration and hyperkeratosis

Etiology & Risk Factors

Caused by dermatophyte overgrowth in warm, humid skin folds. Risk factors include compromised cellular immunity, prolonged antibiotic usage, hyperhidrosis, and direct contact with infected vectors.

Recommended Therapies & Prescriptions

  • Topical Antifungals: Clotrimazole 1% cream or Miconazole
  • Systemic Antifungals: Oral Fluconazole 150mg or Terbinafine
  • Keep affected skin clean and completely dry

Recommended Nutritional Guidelines

Recommended Foods:
  • Probiotic yogurt & fermented kefir
  • Garlic & coconut oil (antifungal lauric acid)
  • Leafy greens & low-glycemic vegetables
Foods to Avoid:
  • Refined sugars & high-carb sweets
  • Yeast-containing bakery products
  • Excessive alcohol & sweetened beverages

Exercise & Exertion Guidelines

  • Light to moderate cardio (walking, indoor cycling)
  • Avoid wearing damp sweaty gym clothing for extended periods
  • Shower immediately following workout sessions

Clinical Precautions & Prevention

  • Do not share personal towels, footwear, or grooming kits
  • Wear loose-fitting breathable cotton clothing
  • Consult physician if rash spreads or develops secondary bacterial infection
